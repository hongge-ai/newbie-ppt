# Bauhaus 1919–1933

## I. Template Overview

An exhibition catalogue for a classroom, talk or illustrated briefing. Let the material determine length, order and repetition. Select a plate for its explanatory job. Dates and milestones belong on a timeline only when the evidence gives a sequence. Short terms or measures can carry a typographic contrast. Replace every sample photograph and caption with evidence relevant to the new subject.

## II. Color Scheme

| Role | Color | Placement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vermilion | #BD4029 | One focal intervention per composition |
| Paper | #F2EEE5 | Continuous warm page ground |
| Ink | #171717 | Headlines, rules and dark timeline background |
| Sand | #E3DCCE | Large supporting geometric fields |
| Secondary | #746E64 | Captions and folios |
| Light | #FFFFFF | Type on dark photographic fields |

## III. Typography

Microsoft YaHei with Arial for figures. Use heavy titles, oversized short anchors and small exhibition captions. Preserve the generous gutters. Rewrite or split excess text before reducing type.

## IV. Signature Design Elements

For the opening portrait use a high-contrast monochrome crop, retaining the vermilion accent. Use a 64 px outer inset, square-edged photos, large aligned type, thin rules, and asymmetrical image/text fields. Keep faces or objects legible in the crop. In documentary compositions darken the left of the replacement photograph sufficiently for light type. In the closing composition use the vermilion disc as the dominant illustration; when a photograph is called for, integrate it into the field around the disc and keep the closing statement legible.
